## Images ^38f477

Most of the time you can just drop an image into your post and it will look great. Like this portrait-oriented image of the Half Dome. It took around 5 hours tracking the moon and waiting for this scene. ^88a5c3

![Moonrise above the Half Dome](post-DSC_1451.77c2fef3.jpg) ^6c0de3

The next shot, a landscape scene at the westernmost point of Europe, also looks nice as the post flows. ^301b8d

![Sunset at Cabo da Roca](post-DSC00103.a6dd71a2.jpg) ^bcfe78

However, if you need more control over the image placement, there are a few knobs. ^3bbf7c

### Wide placement ^e14a53
Not every image wants to sit politely within your text column. Some need room to breathe. Like this photo of Dubai in early morning. ^0f69e6

![Dubai morning](post-DSC09064-Edit.4f9666e8.jpg "placement=wide") ^6747a3

On smaller screens you probably won't notice the difference, but on larger displays, wide placement lets images like this pop. ^87f2fa

### Narrow placement ^ec1b9f
On the other hand, not every photo needs to dominate the page either. Sometimes a quieter presentation lets the image speak for itself. Like this Amsterdam evening view. ^701892

![Amsterdam evening](post-DSC00385.a233b99c.jpg "placement=narrow") ^611600

Narrow placement is default for the portrait-oriented images, so it won't take effect on them. ^81d508

### Fit placement ^01024c

If you want an image to fill the text column width, fit placement is what you need. The Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque in Abu Dhabi, shot before dawn to catch the reflection while the water was still. ^d1ba72

![Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque](post-DSC00502-Edit-Edit.d9f883e9.jpg "placement=fit") ^90cd3c

Fit placement is the default for the landscape-oriented images. ^6b9e11
