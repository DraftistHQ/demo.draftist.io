## Heading Levels ^0c9292

Six levels available. H1 is reserved for the post title, so H2 is your top-level section heading in the post's content. This section you're reading now uses H2—it defines the major parts of your content and creates the primary navigation structure. ^242c61

### H3 Heading ^aa9d34

This is where most of your content structure happens. H3 headings break up major sections into digestible chunks. ^4e83ca

#### H4 Heading ^dcf48f

When you need another level of organization within an H3 section, H4 comes in. Think of it as a sub-topic—useful for breaking down complex subjects without creating a whole new section. ^e9ac97

##### H5 Heading ^7e6e69

You won't use this often, but it's there when you need it. Technical documentation sometimes goes this deep, especially when documenting nested data structures or multi-level configurations. ^b19473

###### H6 Heading ^d32cf0

The deepest level. If you find yourself here regularly, consider restructuring your content. But for edge cases—like detailed API references or deeply nested examples—it exists. ^6253d7

## Text Formatting ^b47859

**Bold text** for emphasis. ^54290f

*Italic text* for subtle emphasis. ^014baa

**_Bold and italic_** together. ^499ad0

~~Strikethrough~~ for deletions. ^ea24e1

`Inline code` for technical terms. ^e01084

Combined: **bold with _italic_ and ~~strikethrough~~ plus `code`**. ^d982f3

### Ordered ^5bd60d

1. First step ^815616
2. Second step ^d2a16c
3. Third step ^99787f

With nesting: ^087416

1. Main point ^f427c6
   - Supporting detail
   - Another detail
2. Second point ^e8cc58
   - Detail here
     1. Numbered sub-item
     2. Another numbered
3. Third point ^b70478

2. Second ordered ^a093c8

     1. Back to ordered
     2. Another ordered
